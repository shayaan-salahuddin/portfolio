Phase 01

Discover - The hospital handover problem

The brief: Design an interactive hospital solution that makes stressful environments clearer, safer, and less overwhelming. Our team chose to focus on clinical handovers, the transfer of patient care between shifts, as a high-stakes, frequently under-designed touchpoint in hospital operations.
Research used a triangulated approach: online ethnography (Reddit forums like r/emergencymedicine and r/doctorsUK, plus TikTok nursing communities), a survey targeting nursing staff, in-depth interviews, and a co-design workshop with healthcare participants to validate our problem framing and explore early design directions.
Stat 01
48% information discrepancy rate
Research found 48% of patients experience information discrepancies during emergency handovers, directly risking patient safety outcomes.
Stat 02
1 in 8 handovers delayed
1 in 8 ambulance-to-ED handovers are delayed by 30–60 minutes, straining resources and creating cascading pressure on nursing staff.
Finding 01
Chaotic, noisy environments
"Handover is always chaotic, too much noise, constant interruptions, and people rushing. It's easy to miss details." Nurses described environments that made structured communication almost impossible.
Finding 02
No standard, no accountability
"Every hospital does it differently. There's no standard, so you're left guessing what the next person actually meant." Verbal-only handovers left no verifiable record.
Finding 03
Frameworks feel rigid
Existing frameworks like ISBAR were seen as helpful in principle but rigid in practice, difficult to adapt to individual patient needs or fast-moving situations.
Finding 04
Patients are excluded
Nurses expressed frustration at not being able to meaningfully involve patients and families in handovers, a relationship dimension being lost to process pressure.
Phase 02

Define - Reframing the design challenge

We synthesised research through affinity diagramming, clustering findings into themes around communication structure, environmental factors, and tool effectiveness. Our primary persona, Cassandra, a 2-year nurse in a Neurological/Stroke Unit at Liverpool Hospital, grounded design decisions in a specific, real-world perspective.
Cassandra struggles to conduct effective handovers in loud, time-pressured environments. She finds the ISBAR framework too rigid to adapt to individual patients, and often needs to clarify incomplete information after handovers. She wants a system that improves completeness, enables patient involvement, and adapts to situational needs.
How might we

"How might we design a solution that helps nurses conduct complete, structured handovers in noisy, time-pressured hospital environments, while giving patients a voice in the process?"

C1
ISBAR as the structural backbone
The ISBAR framework (Introduction, Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) is the Australian clinical standard. Any tool must reinforce it, not replace it, while allowing adaptation per patient.
C2
Speed is non-negotiable
The tool must match or beat the current paper/verbal process in speed. Any added friction becomes a barrier to adoption in time-pressured ward environments.
C3
Patient inclusion as a design goal
A key insight from our co-design workshop: nurses want to build trust with patients, not just complete paperwork. The tool should enable, not crowd out, that relationship.
Phase 03

Develop, Prototyping and user testing

We built a structured tablet interface using the SBAR framework as the information architecture backbone. The outgoing nurse completes each SBAR section sequentially; the incoming nurse sees a patient summary dashboard with colour-coded urgency levels before entering any patient record.
Final Presentation — Canva Open ↗
Early user testing with nursing students revealed that the SBAR accordion design worked well for structured input, but the patient list dashboard needed stronger visual differentiation, colour alone was insufficient for urgency cues given potential colour blindness in clinical staff.
Iteration 01
Added icon + colour for urgency
Combined colour (yellow/red lamp icons) with explicit text labels and shape differentiation to meet WCAG AA contrast requirements and support colour-blind users.
Iteration 02
Auto-populated fields
Patient vitals from the last handover auto-populated into the new session, reducing re-entry time by an estimated 60% per patient.
Phase 04

Deliver - Prototype and reflections

The final mid-fi prototype demonstrates the full handover flow: patient list → urgency triage → SBAR input → handover completion confirmation. Initial testing showed strong potential to reduce both time-to-complete and information omissions.
